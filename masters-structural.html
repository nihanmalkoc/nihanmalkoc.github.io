Lattice Continuum

Structural Optimization

A temporary pavilion designed as a single continuous lattice shell, every beam tuned so the structure carries itself with the least material. Built in Alpaca4d and pushed through Galapagos, the form negotiates three competing goals at once: minimum mass, minimum displacement, and balanced utilization.

  • Lattice shellone continuous beam network
  • Alpaca4d FEAloads, supports, displacement
  • Galapagosmulti-objective optimization

Structural Optimization  ·  MaCAD · IAAC · 2025–26

Form Finding

The shell starts from a simple sequence, a bounding box, control points, a scale, a rotation and a loft, a geometry that later doubles as the brick form. Circulation and the relationship to the bounding box are resolved before any structure is added.

Supports & Loads

The pavilion is anchored by 204 fixed supports at its base and loaded in Alpaca4d, so every optimization run is judged against a realistic set of boundary conditions.

Optimization Goals

The lattice is modelled as beam elements and explored along three goals, minimize total mass, minimize maximum displacement, and balanced utilization, each pulling the design toward a different structural priority.

Manual Iterations & Multi-Objective Search

Successive manual cases trade the variables against each other, adjusting the form, increasing member density and growing beam diameters, each read through mass, displacement and utilization together. Because manual tuning alone couldn't reach the target, the evaluation was handed to Galapagos for a systematic multi-objective search, and the final displacement field is read across the deformed shell.
